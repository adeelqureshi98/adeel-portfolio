5 Things to Check Before Buying a Website

A website is an investment, not a decoration. Before you pay anyone — including me — run through these five checks. They separate a site that brings customers from one that just sits on the internet.

  • Mobile first, always. Most of your customers will open your site on a phone. If it isn't fast and beautiful on mobile, nothing else matters. Ask to see the mobile version before you approve anything.
  • Speed is a feature. A site that takes five seconds to load loses visitors before they see a single word. Good hosting, compressed images, clean code — these aren't technical details, they're business decisions.
  • One clear action per page. Every page should guide the visitor to one thing: call, WhatsApp, order, visit. If a homepage has ten competing buttons, it has zero.
  • Real content, not filler. Your story, your real photos or honest visuals, your actual services and prices approach. Visitors smell placeholder text instantly — and it kills trust.
  • You must own it. Make sure you get full ownership: domain in your name, access to everything, and the ability to update content. A website you can't control is a liability, not an asset.

Get these five right and even a simple website will outperform a flashy one that ignores them. That's the standard I build to — and the standard you should demand from anyone.
